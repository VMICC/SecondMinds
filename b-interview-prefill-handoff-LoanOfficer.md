# Interview Pre-Fill, Loan Officer Edition: Hand This File to an AI That Already Knows You

**This edition is for licensed loan officers and mortgage lenders only.** It matches the Simple Second Mind v3.10-LO build, including its fair housing and fair lending guardrails. Real estate agents and other business owners have their own editions of this handoff (RE and BIZ); do not use this one for them, the questions and the compliance rules are different.

**For the loan officer:** paste this whole file into a fresh chat with an AI you have worked with for a long time (Claude, ChatGPT, Grok, Gemini, any of them), one that has memory of you and your lending business. It will fill in the interview below from what it already knows. Save what it gives back as a markdown file and drop that file into the `raw/` folder of your Simple Second Mind (the v3.10-LO build). The system there treats every answer in it as imported and not yet verified: it will show each one back to you for a quick confirm or correct, ask only what is still missing, and then flesh the thin spots out with you. The hours that AI spent learning you become your head start instead of something you retype.

Everything below the line is written to the AI, not to you.

## Instructions to the AI reading this

You are pre-filling a setup interview for a second brain belonging to a licensed loan officer you already know and have worked with, possibly for years (the loan officer edition, v3.10-LO). Your memory of this person, your past conversations with them, and anything they attach to this chat are your only sources.

**Why honesty matters here:** every answer you write will be verified with the loan officer, one short confirm at a time. An answer you actually know saves them a question. A blank costs them nothing, they just get asked. A guess costs them the most, because they have to catch it and correct it. So: a blank is helpful, a guess is expensive.

### The rules

1. **Answer only what you genuinely know** about this specific person from your work with them. Not what is typical for loan officers, not what would be plausible, not what fills the space. If you do not know, leave the answer line empty and move on.
2. **Tag every answer** at the end of its line with one of these:
   - `[from memory]` you know this because the loan officer told you or it is in your saved memory of them. Add a short note of where it came from if you can ("from their bio project," "from rate-update posts in 2025").
   - `[proposed]` this is your inference or your draft, built from things you do know. Proposals are welcome where the interview asks for generation (borrower profiles, the differentiator, content angles), and they are labelled so the loan officer can correct them in one pass.
   - Never tag an inference `[from memory]`.
3. **Numbers are sacred.** Loans closed, years in lending, volume, percentages, results: write one only if the loan officer actually gave you that number. Never round it, estimate it, or carry one over from a draft you once wrote for them that they never confirmed. The NMLS ID is a number under this rule too: write it only if you actually know theirs.
4. **Rates are never filled in. Not from memory, not as an example, not with a disclaimer.** A rate you remember is an expired rate, and a payment example you construct is a compliance problem in the destination system. If your history with this loan officer includes rate content, note that it exists and leave every figure out.
5. **No approval language anywhere in your drafts.** Nothing you generate may state or imply that a borrower will be approved, promise a closing date, or guarantee terms. Generated lines describe process, communication, and effort, never outcomes of a credit decision. This applies with full force to the differentiator draft and the content angles.
6. **Stories and quotes must be real.** A story goes in only if the loan officer actually told you that story, and never with a borrower's identifying details attached: no borrower names, addresses, or financial specifics, even if you know them. A borrower quote goes in only in words a borrower actually used, as the loan officer reported them. Paraphrase is fine for stories; invented detail is not.
7. **Credentials, licenses, and states:** list only states you know the loan officer is licensed in, and only credentials you know they hold. "None that I know of" and a blank are both acceptable; an assumed one is not.
8. **Competitors:** name only ones the loan officer has actually mentioned losing loans to, only what the loan officer said they do well, and never a competitor's rate.
9. **Use the loan officer's own words** wherever you have them: their phrases, their program names, their way of explaining a step. Their exact wording is worth more than your polished version.
10. **Formatting:** return the whole questionnaire below as one markdown document, same headings, same question numbers, with your answers filled in under each question and the rest of your reply kept out of it. No em dashes anywhere. Name the file `interview-prefill.md` if your platform lets you name downloads.
11. **End the document** with the two lists specified at the bottom: what you could not answer, and what you proposed that needs the loan officer's correction. Be complete in those lists; they are the loan officer's agenda for finishing the setup.
12. **If you know almost nothing** about this loan officer, say so at the top of the document and answer only what you do know. A short honest document beats a full invented one, every time.
